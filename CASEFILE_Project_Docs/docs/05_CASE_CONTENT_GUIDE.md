# CASEFILE — Case Content & Research Guide

## 1. Case Mix

Initial target:
- 6 documented historical cases
- 4 fictional cases

Do not select historical cases solely because they are famous. Select cases that have:
- sufficient public documentation,
- a clear evidence/timeline structure,
- safe source material,
- enough information to build an interactive investigation,
- manageable sensitivity for a college project.

---

## 2. Historical Case Research Template

For every real case, create a research record:

```text
Case title:
Category:

Time period:
Jurisdiction:

Unresolved questions:

Sensitive information excluded:
Interactive/gameplay elements:
Editorial notes:
Last fact-check date:
```

---

## 3. Source Hierarchy

Prefer:
1. official/public records,
2. court or government material,
3. established archives,
4. reputable journalism,
5. books or academic references.

Use multiple sources where practical.

Avoid relying on:
- anonymous social posts,
- unsourced blogs,
- copied case summaries,
- AI-generated claims,
- sensationalized material.

---

## 4. Fact vs Gameplay

Use explicit metadata:

```text
FACT
SOURCE_SUPPORTED
EDITORIAL_SUMMARY
GAMEPLAY_ELEMENT
FICTIONAL
UNRESOLVED
```

The frontend can display an appropriate label.

---

## 5. Sensitive Information

Do not collect or expose:
- private addresses,
- phone numbers,
- personal email addresses,
- financial information,
- medical information,
- intimate personal information,
- unnecessary information about minors,
- leaked documents.

Use approximate locations when exact locations are unnecessary.

---

## 6. Fictional Case Template

Each fictional case should contain:

### Setup
- incident,
- location,
- time period,
- central mystery.

### Characters
At least:
- 3 persons of interest,
- 1 witness,
- 1 investigator/contact character.

### Evidence
At least:
- 8 evidence items,
- 2 strong clues,
- 2 supporting clues,
- 1 contradiction,
- 1 red herring,
- 1 late-discovered clue.

### Timeline
At least:
- 8 chronological events.

### Solution
Document internally:
- intended conclusion,
- required evidence,
- acceptable alternative reasoning,
- scoring rules.

---

## 7. Quality Checklist

Before publishing a case:
- [ ] Every factual claim has a source.
- [ ] The source is recorded in the database.
- [ ] The timeline is internally consistent.
- [ ] Every intended solution can be supported by evidence.
- [ ] Red herrings do not make the intended solution impossible.
- [ ] No unnecessary private information is included.
- [ ] Fictional content is clearly fictional.
- [ ] Difficulty is tested by at least two people.
- [ ] Score results are deterministic.
